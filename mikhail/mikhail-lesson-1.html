Lesson 1: Real Conversations

Bali edition — start talking to people, for real

• 60 minutes
• Mostly speaking — you'll talk way more than you listen to me
• Café · gym · airport · cars · dating
• By the end: a working toolkit for tomorrow

The one skill for today
Most people get stuck because their small talk turns into an interview:

Where are you from? → What do you do? → How long are you here? → Where do you live?

It works much better when you do this instead:
STARTWhere are you from?
REACTOh nice, I've never been!
FOLLOW UPWhat part?
SHAREI've been thinking about visiting Australia.

That's it. That's the whole lesson, applied 20 different ways today.

1 · Cold Start

NO PREP, JUST GO
5 min

Your task

We don't know each other. We're sitting next to each other in a café in Bali.

You have 30 seconds to start a conversation with me. Go.

Don't overthink it. There's no wrong answer here — we'll come back to this exact situation at the end of the lesson.

2 · Your Toolkit

Phrases that actually get used in real conversations
8–10 min
StartingWhen / meaning
How's it going?Casual opener, works anywhere
You from around here?Café, bar, coworking space
Is this your first time in Bali?Touristy places
ReactingWhen / meaning
No way! / Seriously?Genuine surprise
That's actually pretty cool.A real positive reaction
Fair enough. / I know what you mean.Agreement, relating to them
Following upWhen / meaning
How come?Quick, natural — better than a flat "why"
What was that like?Invites them to say more
How did you get into that?About a hobby, job, or interest
When you need a secondWhen / meaning
Sorry, I didn't catch that.You missed something
Could you say that again?Ask them to repeat
I've never heard that word before.Totally normal, own it

2.5 · Save the Conversation

The conversation is dying. Save it.
6–7 min

Michael: Where are you from?
Person: London.
Michael: How long have you been here?
Person: Two weeks.
Michael: Do you like Bali?
Person: Yeah.

Your task
Use: react → comment → follow up → share something about yourself.
Then we'll do it three more times — gym, airport, café — and each time the conversation will start to die. Your job is to keep it alive.

4 · The Picture

Imagine you're in this place
7 min

Your teacher will show you a photo — a café or beach club scene.

  • Who would you talk to? Why this person?
  • How would you start?
  • What could you ask them? What could you say about yourself?
  • What do you think the people in the photo are actually talking about? Continue their conversation.

5 · Speed Small Talk

5 ROUNDS, 1–2 MINUTES EACH
10 min

Your teacher will become a new person every round. Just react to whoever's in front of you — you don't know what they'll say.

Round 1 — Café
Someone's sitting near you in a coffee shop. Start a conversation.
Round 2 — Gym
Someone's working out nearby. Start a conversation — skip "what do you do?", find something more interesting.
Round 3 — Airport
Your flight is delayed. The person next to you is in the same situation. Talk.
Round 4 — Cars
This person is into cars too. Keep it a real conversation — not just a monologue about your own car.
Round 5 — First Time in Bali
This person just arrived in Bali. Tell them something useful — and ask about their own travels too.

6 · Mini Date

No script — just try it
8–10 min
The scene
You're at a bar / café in Bali. You've just met a girl.
She seems interested — but she won't carry the conversation for you.
Run the first 5 minutes yourself. No script — just try it.

After round 1, we'll talk about:

  • Did it feel natural? Did you ask too many questions?
  • Did you tell her anything about yourself?
  • Where did it feel awkward?

7 · Final Challenge

Same as the start
5 min

We're strangers in a café in Bali.

Start a conversation.

No hints this time.

Then let's compare

  • Was it easier this time? What changed?
  • Which phrases actually helped?
  • What do you still want to work on?

Homework

Pick one
Option A
Start one short conversation in English in real life on Bali. Write down: who you talked to, what you talked about, what was easy/hard, one phrase you wished you'd known.
Option B
Send a 1–2 minute voice message: "Tell me about a conversation you had recently and how you could have made it more interesting."
