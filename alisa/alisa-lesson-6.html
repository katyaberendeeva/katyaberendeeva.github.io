Wedding Traditions
Around the World 💍

A curious, people-first conversation about the meaning behind wedding traditions — and the celebration Alice would love to create. A2–B1 · speak, react, ask follow-up questions.

02 · article & video chat

Traditions with a story 🌍

12 min

Think back to the Vogue article and the short wedding-traditions video you watched before class. Look at each photo, explain what you remember, then share your reaction. You don't need perfect details.

Photo cards are visual prompts representing the traditions, rather than reproductions of Vogue's photographs. Tap a prompt to reveal language support.

💃 The money dance

Guests take turns dancing with the newlyweds and give or pin money as a wish for prosperity.

Your take: Does this feel generous, fun, awkward, or a mix?

Recall & opinion prompts

Where is this tradition practised? How can the money support a couple? Would you join the dance? It's a way to … / I would feel … because …

🧹 Jumping the broom

The couple jumps over a broom, a symbol of sweeping away past wrongs and beginning a new chapter.

Your take: What could this symbol mean to a couple today?

Recall & opinion prompts

The video says the custom originated in Ghana and is still practised there; some African American couples include it as a tribute to tradition. What does the broom symbolise? To me, it represents …

🪒 The Greek koumbaro

A close friend helps the groom shave and get ready on the wedding morning — a gesture of trust.

Your take: Which friend would you trust with an important role?

Recall & opinion prompts

The koumbaro is similar to a best man. Why might getting ready together feel meaningful? It shows that … / I'd choose … because …

🎉 The fiancés' parade

Before the wedding, friends cover one or both fiancés in things like soot, feathers, or food, then parade them through town.

Your take: Would you laugh, feel embarrassed, or say “no thanks”?

Recall & opinion prompts

The tradition was described as protection from supernatural danger. Is the meaning more important than the mess? I think it's … / I wouldn't mind …

Useful ways to react and keep the conversation going

That's surprising! · I didn't know that. · It sounds like … · I can see why people enjoy it. · What does it represent? · Would people still do this today?

04 · build questions

Ask, follow up, listen 👂

10 min

Take turns. Choose a question stem, finish it, and ask a follow-up based on the answer. Keep it natural; don't read a script.

Question starters
What kind of wedding …?
Would you ever …?
Which tradition …?
How would you feel if …?
Who would you ask to …?
What would make … special?
Follow-up toolkit
Why do you think so?
Can you tell me more?
Has that always been your opinion?
What about your family?
How is that different from …?
Pair task: Ask four original questions about weddings, traditions, or celebrations. After each answer, ask one follow-up question before you share your own view.
Example conversation

A: Which tradition would you include in your wedding?
B: Maybe the money dance. It sounds joyful.
A: Why do you think so? Would you use the money for anything special?

05 · personal experience

People, plans & celebrations ✨

10 min

Pick any three prompt cards. Answer with a small story, an example, or an explanation. Passing on a question is always okay.

Tell me about a birthday or celebration you enjoyed. What went well?
🎭 A memorable performance
Have you performed, competed, or supported a friend on stage? What happened?
🎥 A video you enjoyed
Tell me about a video, creator, or performance you watched recently. What did you like about it?
💐 A lovely wedding moment
Have you seen a wedding moment in real life, a film, or online that stayed with you?
🎶 Your favourite party detail
What makes a party feel special: music, food, guests, decorations, or something else?
🌱 A new experience

Story support: keep it easy

When? … · Who was there? … · What happened? … · How did you feel? … · Why do you remember it? …
It happened … / I was with … / The best part was … / I felt … because …

06 · final challenge

Design your dream wedding 💍

13 min

Plan a wedding that feels like you. Talk your way through these choices, then present your celebration in about two minutes.

1 · The feeling

Small and cosy, big and lively, outdoors, by the sea, at home, or somewhere surprising?

2 · The people

Who must be there? Who will have a special role?

3 · The traditions

Choose one tradition from today, a tradition from your family, or invent a new one. What does it mean?

4 · The details

What will guests eat, listen to, wear, or do? Add one personal touch.

Present your dream wedding: “My wedding would be … I would invite … One tradition I'd include is … because … The best part would be …” Then answer one curious follow-up question.
Extra support: mix and match

I'd love a … wedding in … · I'd keep it simple / colourful / relaxed. · I would include … because it reminds me of … · My guests could … · I wouldn't want … · The most important thing would be …

Stretch it to B1

Compare two options with whereas or while; explain a choice with although; or imagine a change with If I had …, I would …
