Cake idioms 🍰

8 min

These expressions are not usually about real cake. Learn the meaning from the example.

a piece of cakeочень легко

“The test was a piece of cake.”

have your cake and eat it tooхотеть получить сразу всё, в том числе несовместимое

“You can’t have your cake and eat it too.”

the icing on the cakeприятное дополнение к чему-то хорошему

“The free tickets were the icing on the cake.”

sell like hot cakesпродаваться очень быстро

“The concert tickets sold like hot cakes.”

Remember: “a piece of cake” = easy. It does not mean a piece of dessert in this sentence.

04 · reading

A birthday to remember

8 min
Short article · A2–B1

Not just a birthday cake

Last Saturday, Mia turned fifteen. She didn’t want a huge party, so her family planned a small surprise at home. Her brother invited six friends and decorated the living room with balloons. Mia’s mum made a chocolate cake, and everyone brought a little gift.

At first, Mia thought the evening would be quiet. But when she opened the door, her friends shouted, “Surprise!” She laughed, made a wish and blew out the candles. Later, they played music and took funny photos. Mia said the party was a piece of cake to organise because everyone helped. The best part was not the presents; it was spending time with people she loved. The homemade cake was the icing on the cake.

C · True, False or Not stated?

Choose True if the text says it, False if the text says the opposite, or Not stated if there is not enough information.

Open the answer key

1. False — she turned fifteen. 2. True. 3. Not stated. 4. True — she says the best part was spending time with people she loved.

05 · dialogue

At a birthday party 💬

10 min

Read the conversation aloud twice. First, read it as written. Then change the details and act it out.

LILY: Happy birthday, Sam! This is for you.

SAM: Thank you! You didn’t have to get me anything.

LILY: I hope you like it. Are you having a good time?

SAM: Yes, it’s great! The games are fun, and the cake is amazing.

LILY: Do you want to make a wish before you blow out the candles?

SAM: Of course! I’m not telling anyone what I wished for.

LILY: Fair enough! By the way, organising this party must have been hard.

SAM: Not really. With my friends helping, it was a piece of cake!

Useful expressions
Happy birthday! · This is for you. · I hope you like it. · Are you having a good time? · Fair enough! · You didn’t have to! · Thanks for inviting me.

06 · listening · culture corner 1 · ex. 1 · p. 21

Pow-wow: a celebration of Native American culture

8–10 min
1 · Before listening

Look at the picture and the title. What might you see at this celebration? Choose two ideas: traditional clothes · dancing · a market · snow · a boat race.

2 · Listen — Audio 09 · Culture Corner 1, Exercise 1, page 21

Listen once without stopping. Focus on the general topic: what kind of event is it?

3 · Task A — Choose the correct answer

  1. The Albuquerque Pow-wow takes place in…
       
  2. The dancers represent…
       
  3. The event begins with…
       
  4. At the market, visitors can buy…
       

Task B — Answer in 1–2 sentences

  1. What can visitors see and hear at the Pow-wow?
  2. Would you like to visit this event? Why / why not?
4 · Listen again

Play the recording again. Check the place, who takes part, how the event begins, and what people can find at the market.

5 · Check answers

Task A: 1. New Mexico. 2. Many Native American tribes. 3. A Grand Entry of Dancers. 4. Traditional crafts and jewellery.

Task B — sample answers: Visitors can see traditional dancing, colourful clothes and hear music. They can also visit a market with crafts, jewellery and food. / I’d like to visit because it looks colourful and I want to learn about the culture.

6 · Discuss

1. Which part of the Pow-wow sounds most interesting to you: the dancing, the music or the market?

2. What traditional celebration in your country could you introduce to a visitor?
