🐶 Life with a Labrador

Daily routines, habits & funny stories

Lesson overview: 60 min. Builds on Lesson 1 (Present Simple vs Past Simple, daily routine, irregular verbs). New focus: Present Simple + adverbs of frequency, light PS review. Liza — 20yo, massage therapist + factory worker, has a labrador, above 5th-grade vocabulary.

☕ Warm-up 5 min

Casual catch-up, no correction — just get her talking.

  • How was your week? Are you tired after work?
  • What do you usually do after work?
  • Did anything funny happen this week?
  • → And what about your dog?

🐶 Speaking — Meet My Labrador 10 min

Tell me about your dog. Move through the tiers — you don't need every question, follow what she says.

Easy
  • What's your dog's name? How old is he/she?
  • What does he/she look like?
  • What does he/she like?
Routine
  • What does your dog usually do in the morning?
  • How often do you walk your dog?
  • Where does your dog usually sleep?
  • What does your dog do when you're working? What does he/she usually eat?
Personal
  • What's the funniest thing your dog does?
  • What does your dog hate? What does your dog always do?
  • What's one thing you'd like to change?
Follow-ups: "Why do you think he does that?" / "Does he do that every day?" / "How does that make you feel?" — push for one extra sentence each time.

🔄 Grammar — Adverbs of Frequency 10 min

ALWAYS100%
USUALLY80%
OFTEN60%
SOMETIMES40%
RARELY10%
NEVER0%

Position: before most verbs — I usually work late.  |  after be — My dog is always tired.

Practice A — reorder:

1. I walk my dog in the morning. (usually)I usually walk my dog in the morning.
2. He sits still when I work. (never)He never sits still when I work.
3. She is hungry before dinner. (always)She is always hungry before dinner.
4. We go for a long walk on Sundays. (sometimes)We sometimes go for a long walk on Sundays.
5. He barks at strangers. (often)He often barks at strangers.

Practice B — make it personal: I always... / I usually... / I often... / I sometimes... / I rarely... / I never...

Watch for: adverb placed after main verb ("I walk usually") or before "be" ("I am usually" said correctly but "I usually am" as error). Gently model, don't over-correct mid-flow.
