03 · reading

Five festivals you may not know 📖

15 min

Read the adapted article. Try to understand the main idea first. You do not need to translate every word.

Before you read
Look at the names and photos. Which festival do you think is about food? Which one is about winter? Which one includes a race?
Spain · June

1. El Colacho: The Baby Jumping Festival

Every June, people in bright devil costumes jump over babies who lie on soft mattresses. This tradition started hundreds of years ago. Local people believe that the jump protects the babies from evil and brings them good luck. Families watch carefully while drums play in the streets. It is a very unusual festival, but it is an important part of local culture.

Key idea: a traditional festival that is believed to protect babies.

Spain · August

2. La Tomatina: The Tomato Fight

Every August, the small town of Buñol becomes red. Thousands of people throw ripe tomatoes at each other in the streets. The festival began in the 1940s during a local celebration. Today, people travel from many countries to take part. It is messy, loud and fun. After the fight, people clean the streets and share food with friends.

Key idea: people celebrate by throwing tomatoes.

United Kingdom · May

3. Cooper’s Hill Cheese Rolling

In this strange race, a large round cheese rolls down a steep hill. Competitors run after it, trying to catch it or reach the bottom first. People often fall because the hill is very steep. The tradition has existed for a long time, and many visitors come to watch. It looks funny, but it can also be dangerous!

Key idea: competitors race down a hill after a wheel of cheese.

Mexico · December

4. Night of the Radishes

In the city of Oaxaca, artists turn large radishes into tiny works of art. They carve scenes of people, animals and traditional stories into the vegetables. The festival began as a way to attract people to a Christmas market. Now, artists compete to create the most interesting designs. It shows how creative people can be with simple materials.

Key idea: artists carve radishes into works of art.

China · January

5. Harbin Ice and Snow Festival

Every winter, the city of Harbin becomes a world of ice. Artists build huge ice buildings, towers and sculptures. At night, colourful lights make them look magical. The weather is extremely cold, but many visitors come to see the beautiful art. Children play in the snow while families walk between the shining ice buildings.

Key idea: giant ice sculptures and colourful lights turn winter into a show.

Reading tip 💡
Look for the repeated details: where the festival happens, when it happens, what people do and why it is special.

Form

Your mini-presentation

  2. It takes place in…
  3. It happens in…
  4. People…
  5. I think it is unusual / exciting / beautiful because…
  6. I would / wouldn’t like to visit it because…

Discuss with a partner

D · Writing: an article about a festival ✍️

Task: An international teenage magazine wants an article about a popular festival in your country. Write 120–180 words. You can also choose one festival from today’s lesson.

Plan your article:

  1. Paragraph 1: What is the name of the festival? When, where and why does it take place?
  2. Paragraph 2: What preparations do people make?
  3. Paragraph 3: What happens on the actual day?
  4. Paragraph 4: How do people feel before, during and after the festival? What do you like most about it?
